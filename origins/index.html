The real beginning

It all started with a very simple idea

This page should not hide the process. It should reveal it. That is why the editorial narrative is accompanied by raw material: notes, questions, dialogue and original reasoning.

The first thought

The idea started with a practical question: how can two apps be featured without turning everything into advertising? The answer was to build stories where the characters naturally use the apps to solve real situations.

That is where ImmoBit and AutoBit stop being merely products and become storytelling tools.

Original notes · The first formulation of the idea 
I have decided
that the best way to promote my apps
is to create a comic in which the main characters
use them to solve situations.
The style I have chosen, considering the target audience,
20 to 60 years old, men and women,
should be European ligne claire.
I am not a comic book writer or dialogue writer.
I would just like the theme to evolve from the release
of the first installment, if we can call it that.
Perhaps we will reach 5, 10 or 20 episodes; I do not know.
The episodes do not need to follow a strict timeline;
perhaps that could be reserved for the final two or three.
The apps involved are:
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.memmolalabs.immobit
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.memmolalabs.autobit
Tell me what you think.

Choosing the visual style

The visual direction came from a deliberate choice: European ligne claire. A readable, mature, professional, non-photorealistic style suited to stories about homes, cars, technology and small details.

The world is illustrated. The app is precise.

This rule keeps the project away from photo comics and gives it a recognizable identity as a European tech graphic novel.

Narrative philosophy

No product placement

The point is not to showcase features. It is to show decisions, doubts, risks and details that change everything.

The apps as storytelling tools

ImmoBit and AutoBit should enter the story the way a notebook, an archive, a camera or a professional instrument would: without demanding attention, yet becoming essential.

Original notes · Avoid product placement 
The key point: DO NOT do product placement.

Here is the difference between:

a project people ignore
and something that could genuinely grow.

The apps should not look like:

“Look at my app.”

They should look like:

“This character naturally uses this tool.”

Almost like:

Batman → Batcomputer
detective → archive
pilot → instruments
journalist → camera

Your apps should become:

storytelling tools.
Worldbuilding

Realistic contemporary Europe

The story world is not science fiction. It is a more observant version of our world: European cities, real apps, everyday problems, discreet technology and subtle tension.

The atmosphere

The stories should feel like short, grounded thrillers: silence, light rain, modern cafés, real estate offices, car dealerships, underground parking, tablets and documents.

Characters

First the method, then the faces

Characters begin with a narrative purpose: one person notices things, another asks questions, another introduces conflict. Elena and Luca emerge from there.

Elena Moretti

Elena reads properties as though they were physical memories. Walls, smells, light, silence and hidden details all become signals.

ImmoBit is her method: checklists, notes, scores, comparisons, reminders and reports.

Luca Romano

Luca reads cars the way Elena reads houses. Wear, documents, engines, paintwork, interiors and sellers all tell a story.

AutoBit is his technical memory: assessments, comparisons, reports and reminders.

The initial character structure

At first there was no need for a huge cast. What mattered was a simple dynamic: someone who can read the details, and someone who represents the audience's doubts.

Pilot episode

The first story had to be simple

There was no need to start with a sprawling saga. One strong, familiar situation was enough: a perfect apartment, perhaps a little too perfect.

The perfect apartment

The first narrative outline was structured like a micro-thriller: appearance, doubt, observation, verification, discovery and a final line.

Original notes · Pilot episode structure 
The PERFECT structure to get started
Episode 1
“The perfect apartment”

A very simple plot:

a couple visits an incredible apartment
the price is strangely low
everything looks perfect
the protagonist notices inconsistent details
uses ImmoBit
discovers a problematic history
the sale falls through
an ambiguous final panel

The end.

Shared universe

Separate at first, connected over time

ImmoBit and AutoBit need to grow as two independent series. The crossover only works once readers have learned to recognize both worlds.

The narrative collision

Elena and Luca are looking for the same thing in different markets: the truth behind appearances. When they meet, the crossover is not a gimmick but a natural consequence.

Rhythm and channels

YouTube channels remain separate for ImmoBit and AutoBit. Other social platforms can serve as Memmola Labs hubs for behind-the-scenes posts, concept art, short scenes, UI details and worldbuilding.

Original notes · Suggested publishing rhythm 
Suggested structure

YouTube → separate channels

1 ImmoBit channel
1 AutoBit channel

Publishing:

1 episode per week per channel

So:

Tuesday → ImmoBit
Friday → AutoBit

This is PERFECT because:

each channel stays consistent
the algorithm understands the subject
the audience knows what to expect
you never disappear for a whole week
you have a realistic amount of time to produce the work.
